# Whey Protein: Ethical Consumption Assessment

> **Status:** Working decision, 18 September 2026. Ratings reflect the evidence found so far; they are not permanent certifications.

## Main conclusion: the production model we prefer

The **Beaufort AOP model** is currently the clearest example of the direction we prefer for animal-derived whey. Its main value is not simply that it is traditional or protected by an AOP label. Its value is that production is **bounded by a place and its natural capacity**: a defined mountain region, suitable land, locally adapted Tarine and Abondance cattle, seasonal grazing, winter hay, farmers and controlled milk production.

The ethical principle is:

> **Demand must adapt to the humane and ecological capacity of the production system. Animals, farmers and land must not be pushed harder simply because the market wants more.**

This means:

- **Animal welfare comes before maximum output.** Cows should have adequate pasture, suitable feed, shelter, health care, rest and a life that allows natural behaviour. Milk yield must not be increased by continually placing more physical pressure on the animals.
- **Land and local ecosystems set the limit.** The number of animals and the amount produced should be governed by available pasture, locally produced hay, climate, biodiversity and the labour needed to care for the animals properly.
- **Running out is acceptable.** If ethically produced cheese or whey is sold out, the answer should be to wait, reduce consumption or use a plant alternative—not weaken the rules, crowd in more animals or force higher yields.
- **Useful preservation is preferable to waste.** Turning limited seasonal milk into durable products such as cheese, and drying the resulting cheesemaking whey, allows more of the available milk to be used without requiring constant overproduction. For this reason, whey recovered as a genuine cheesemaking by-product is preferable to native whey produced directly from additional milk, although buying any dairy by-product still supports the dairy economy.
- **Broader demand should be met through replication, not extraction.** One region should not be expanded beyond its carrying capacity. Instead, similarly bounded and humane systems can be developed in other suitable regions—including Sweden—using locally appropriate breeds, feed, land and climate. Each region would serve only the amount of demand that it can support responsibly.
- **The goal is sufficient distributed abundance, not unlimited supply.** Several small or regional systems can together serve a reasonable population while remaining within their own natural limits. This is different from scaling one successful system until its ethical and ecological qualities are lost.

Beaufort AOP is therefore a **promising structural model**, not yet proof of complete animal welfare. Its rules establish matters such as region, breeds, feed and production constraints, but further evidence is still needed about calf treatment and cow–calf separation, housing and space, reproduction, dehorning, veterinary practice, cow longevity, transport and slaughter. An AOP label cannot replace farm-level welfare verification.

## Purpose and ethical standard

Whey remains the current nutritional exception while meat, poultry and ordinary dairy consumption are being reduced or stopped. The aim is not merely to find a legal, Swedish, organic or well-marketed product. A source should show credible evidence of:

- farm and milk origin;
- animal living conditions and welfare;
- feed and grazing practices;
- transport and slaughter/end-of-life practices where relevant;
- environmental management;
- processing and supply-chain traceability; and
- honest, good-faith disclosure that can be independently checked.

When evidence is missing, the product stays unverified. A vegan product avoids the direct dairy-animal-welfare issue, but its crops, labour, processing and environmental impact should still be assessed.

## Level system

| Level | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Level 0 — Unverified baseline** | Current or existing consumption for which the necessary ethical evidence has not been established. Use up existing stock to avoid waste, but do not repurchase by default. |
| **Level 1 — Some positive disclosure** | Better-than-baseline claims or traceability exist, but the evidence is incomplete for this project's ethical standard. A possible interim option, not an approved endpoint. |
| **Level 2 — Credible transitional choice** | Meaningful sourcing and production information is available and the company appears to be acting in good faith. Still requires follow-up on unresolved animal-welfare, environmental or audit questions. |
| **Level 3 — Strongly verified** | Reserved for unusually transparent sourcing backed by farm-level detail, meaningful welfare/environmental standards and credible independent verification. **No reviewed whey brand has yet earned this level.** |

## Brand assessments by level

### Level 0 — Body Science

**Decision:** Finish existing stock; do not repurchase unless new evidence materially changes the assessment.

Body Science is familiar, affordable and its Chocolate and Forest Berries flavours are liked. However, the earlier review did not establish sufficiently specific farm-level milk origin, animal-welfare conditions, grazing/feed standards, end-of-life treatment or independently verifiable supply-chain information. Product quality and Swedish retail availability do not answer those ethical questions.

**Why Level 0:** It remains an **unverified baseline**, not a finding that abuse has been proven. The problem is that the evidence required by this project is missing.

### Level 1 — Nutri&Co

**Decision:** Better than Body Science on disclosed sourcing, but not the preferred purchase.

Nutri&Co publicly states that its native whey comes directly from fresh milk and is sourced from French grass-fed cows. These are useful improvements in origin and production disclosure. However, “grass-fed” alone does not establish the full welfare picture: housing, grazing duration, calf separation, transport, veterinary practices and the eventual fate of cows and calves still need stronger evidence.

**Why Level 1:** Positive and relevant disclosure exists, but it does not yet satisfy the project's full good-faith animal-welfare and traceability standard.

### Level 2 — Protero

**Decision:** A credible transitional candidate from the earlier comparison, but secondary to Protéalpes for the present purchase.

The initial review judged Protero to provide materially better sourcing information than the Level 0 and Level 1 options. It was therefore treated as a plausible ethical step upward rather than a fully verified endpoint.

**Why Level 2:** The disclosure appeared substantial enough for a transitional rating, while gaps remained that prevented Level 3. Before purchase, the exact product page, milk origin, welfare evidence, certifications and delivery conditions should be rechecked and linked here.

### Level 2 — Protéalpes

**Decision:** **Chosen supplier for the first replacement order.**

Protéalpes offered the best practical balance found in the review between improved traceability, apparent good-faith disclosure, suitable whey and plant products, flavours, affordability and delivery to Sweden. Buying both dairy and plant powder from the same supplier also makes a gradual, measurable reduction in animal-derived protein practical.

**Why Level 2:** It was the strongest current transitional choice, but the rating does not mean every part of the dairy system has been independently verified. It should be reassessed when stronger farm-level or third-party evidence becomes available.

## Whey versus plant protein

Whey generally stimulates muscle-protein synthesis efficiently per gram because it is rich in essential amino acids and leucine and is quickly digested. A well-formulated plant blend or sufficient pea-based protein can also support muscle gain, but may require a somewhat larger serving to supply comparable essential amino acids.

For this reason, the transition begins with **35 g whey plus 35–40 g plant powder**, rather than immediately replacing whey gram-for-gram without testing the product. The important practical checks are:

- total daily protein remains near the target;
- servings provide adequate essential amino acids across the day;
- strength, recovery and body weight do not decline unintentionally; and
- digestion and taste remain acceptable enough for consistent use.

The ethical advantage of the plant portion is substantial because it directly reduces demand for dairy production. It does not automatically make the product fully ethical; crop sourcing, labour, processing, packaging and transport still matter.

## Questions for future review

1. Can Protéalpes provide named farms or cooperatives and auditable farm-level welfare standards?
2. How many days per year do supplying cows graze, and how are they housed outside that period?
3. What are the policies on calves, dehorning, antibiotic use, transport and end-of-life slaughter?
4. Are environmental claims supported by measured emissions, energy, water and packaging data?
5. What are the exact crop origins, certifications and labour safeguards for the organic plant protein?
6. After the trial, did 35–40 g/day of plant protein maintain digestion, recovery, strength and weight?
7. Should the next order move beyond 50% plant protein?
8. Does Protéalpes whey actually come from a bounded cheesemaking system comparable to Beaufort AOP, or is Beaufort only a model for future sourcing?
9. Can suitable Swedish dairy regions reproduce the same principles without importing rules that do not fit Swedish land, breeds or climate?
